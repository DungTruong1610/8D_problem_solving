## Slide 4 - Who needs 8D - 1:15

> The video asked whether the problem is the engineer or the blank page. To answer that, let us clarify the 8D problem.
>
> The automotive industry has **three participants**: component manufacturers such as Bosch and Denso; vehicle manufacturers such as Volkswagen, Ford, Toyota and **VinFast**; and buyers, from individual consumers to fleets such as Hertz and Uber.
>
> When a vehicle manufacturer finds a **defective component**, it **asks the component manufacturer to submit an 8D report**. 8D Copilot supports that component manufacturer.
>
> Next. An 8D report answers **five questions**: what is wrong, have defective products been contained, **why did it happen**, how will it be corrected, and how will recurrence be prevented? The third question is the hardest.

## Slide 9 - Challenges - 0:30 - handoff

> Bringing AI into 8D presents five challenges. Next: AI can invent information. Next: it can blame people unfairly. Next: it can retrieve an irrelevant old case. Next: it may not know when to stop. Next: factory data can be very messy.

## Slide 10 - Data - 0:45

> AI is only as trustworthy as the structure of its data. We divide the data into **three groups**.
>
> Next. **Group 1, master data:** machine codes, material codes, defect codes and batches. These codes are **selected from controlled lists, not typed manually**, giving AI a fixed frame of reference.
>
> Next. **Group 2, operational evidence:** today's defect record, inspection measurements, batch history, FMEA and closed cases. Every table **links back to a defect record ID**.
>
> Next. **Group 3, the 8D record:** D1-D8, evidence for each action, who approved it and when. **Every approval leaves a trace.**
>
> Next. The demo uses **one SQLite file** for simplicity, without installing a database server; production deployment uses **PostgreSQL**.

## Slide 11 - Three control layers - 0:45

> On top of this data structure are **three control layers** addressing those challenges.
>
> **GraphAI** guards against irrelevant precedents. It retrieves cases through **actual relationships**: the same machine, material and defect code. "Scratches on a milling machine" and "scratches on a lathe" sound similar but describe different failures. A case that is not similar enough is not returned.
>
> **AI Referee** checks bias toward blaming people. A second AI is **not shown the engineer's conclusion**; it sees only the measurements. If the engineer says "people" but the measurements indicate "machine," the system **stops at D4** and requests evidence.
>
> **AI Stop** prevents AI from exceeding its role: **eight stop rules in three categories**. Unverified facts, such as a measurement stored only in a sentence, require an engineer. A lack of sufficiently similar precedents requires an expert. Decisions beyond AI's authority, such as costs of **EUR 10,000 or more** or changes to SOPs or FMEA, require a Quality Manager or process owner. Rules trigger the stops, and responses enter the audit log.
>
